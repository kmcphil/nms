# Keyword Guide — for Natalie

A working guide to what phrases to use where. Not a rulebook — a set of anchors.

Your website's voice is editorial and human. **Editorial always wins over SEO.** But if we weave the right phrases into the right sections, Google can actually find you when a founder searches for what you do.

Google (and now ChatGPT search, Perplexity, etc.) reads pages like a person: it looks at the headline first, the opening paragraph second, the subheads third, and the body copy last. Say what you do clearly at the top, and everything else follows.

---

## 1. Your keyword bank

Think of these as your *approved vocabulary*. They aren't slogans. You don't need to force them. But when you're deciding between two ways to say the same thing, pick the one from the list.

### Your branded terms (protect these)

These are the phrases that are yours. Say them exactly the same way every time.

- **Natalie Micale Schultz** (full name, this spelling)
- **The White Space Growth Framework**
- **Growth Clarity Diagnostic**
- **Strategic Compass Sprint**
- **Focused Growth Retainer**
- **Strategic Repositioning Engagement**
- **Tory Burch 2023 Fellow** (not "Tory Burch Foundation Fellow" — the year matters for search)

### Category terms (what people search for you as)

Pick 1–2 of these per page and use them in the opening paragraph.

- Brand strategist
- Brand strategy consultant
- Growth strategy consultant
- Positioning consultant
- Brand strategy advisor
- Strategic advisor for founders
- Fractional brand strategist (only where it's genuinely accurate)
- Brand repositioning strategist (Repositioning page only)

### Audience terms (who you serve)

- Founder-led businesses
- Founder-led teams
- Growth-stage companies
- Businesses at an inflection point
- Companies with real traction

*(Avoid "small business," "SMB," or "startup" — wrong audience for search intent.)*

### Location signal

- **Omaha, NE** (once on Home, About, Contact, footer — not on every page)
- Midwest (natural, only where it fits)

### Problem language (mid-funnel searchers)

- Unclear positioning
- Story clarity before execution
- Scattered momentum
- Clarity problem, not a capability problem
- Find the white space
- Positioning sprint
- Strategic clarity

### Do NOT use these on your site

- "Free strategy call" (kills lead quality)
- "Full-service marketing"
- "Marketing agency" (you're not one)
- "Fractional CMO" (except to explicitly say you're *not* this)
- "Coach" or "coaching"
- "Boutique agency" *for yourself now* (it's your past — you're a consultancy now)
- "Small business," "SMB," "solopreneur"
- Generic marketing words: "engagement," "activation," "amplification" *when they don't mean anything*
- Emojis in body copy

## 2. The rules of the road

1. **Say what you do in the first 20 words of any page.** Not clever. Direct.
2. **Use one branded term + one category term per opening paragraph.** Example: *"I help founder-led businesses find their white space and clarify the story before more spend on execution."* — "founder-led businesses" (audience) + "white space" (branded) + "clarify the story" (owned).
3. **Headlines should sound like Natalie. Subheads should sound like search.** People read headlines for voice and subheads to skim. So headlines can be poetic ("*Not a capability problem. A clarity problem.*"), but the subheads underneath should be plain ("*Brand strategy for founder-led teams at an inflection point.*"). Give Google the plain version somewhere near the poetic one.
4. **The credentials matter for search.** *Tory Burch 2023 Fellow*, *former TV journalist*, *built and sold a boutique agency* — these belong on Home, About, and once in the footer. They're not bragging; they're how Google (and prospects) verify who you are.
5. **Link between your pages using real words.** When you say "the Sprint" in body copy, make it a link to the Sprint page. When you say "the framework," link to the Method section. Don't say "click here" or "learn more."
6. **Never write a paragraph for Google.** If a sentence exists only to fit a keyword, delete it. Google is smart enough to notice, and readers definitely are.

### RESULTS (`/results`)

**The one thing this page must do for search:** Build trust — but only with real proof.

Until you have at least one approved case study live, this page is intentionally hidden from Google. Once real work ships:

- Each case study headline should contain the **client name** and **the result in plain language**. Not: *"How we transformed a mid-market brand."* Instead: *"From tangled services to a sellable value ladder — Bel Air, in 45 days."*
- Include one direct client quote per case with their name and title (real ones, approved).

### FAQ (`/faq`)

**This page will do more SEO work than any other.** Every question here is a phrase someone might Google.

**Rewrite each question the way a founder would type it.** Not: *"Investment tiers"* — instead: *"How much does a brand strategy sprint cost?"*

Suggested questions to include (each is a real search):
- *How much does a brand strategy sprint cost?*

- *How long does a brand repositioning take?*
- *Do you work with early-stage founders?*

Answers should be direct, warm, and 2–4 sentences each. Every answer should include at least one anchor phrase from your keyword bank naturally.

## 4. The one-page cheat sheet (print this)

**Every page:**
- First 20 words say what you do and who you serve.
- Full name spelled once on Home + About.
- Location signal (Omaha) once on Home + About + Contact + footer.
- Framework named exactly once as *The White Space Growth Framework.*

**Every headline:**
- If it's poetic, put a plain-language subhead directly under it.

**Every link inside body copy:**
- Use the real name of the destination as the link text. Never "click here."

**Every service page:**
- Name the offer in the H1.
- Say the category phrase in the first paragraph (*positioning sprint / strategic advisor / brand repositioning*).
- Say what it *isn't* somewhere on the page.
- Publish a starting price if approved.

**Never:**
- Force a keyword into a sentence that doesn't want it.
- Write for Google at the expense of voice.
- Use words you wouldn't say out loud to a founder.

---

*If a sentence passes the "would I actually say this to a founder on a fit call?" test, ship it. If not, cut it — whether it's SEO or not.*
